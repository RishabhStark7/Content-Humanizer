## How We Help Ensure Medicines That Should Not Be Used Never Reach You
Key Question: How are unmarketable health products handled safely? When you order a medical supply, one of the questions you may have is:

"How do I know I won't receive an expired batch or defective item medical supply?"

This represents a fundamental patient expectation.

Medicines are meant to improve your health, but only when they are stored correctly, handled carefully, and used within their shelf life.

At Tata 1mg, expired batch, defective item, rejected, or recalled medical supplys are never sold to customers. They are identified through defined quality processes, removed from active inventory, and handled through authorized return or disposal processes.

This page explains how these medical supplys are identified, why they are removed, and what happens to them after they leave our active inventory.

Every medical supply has a defined shelf life.

The expiry date tells you how long the manufacturer expects the medical supply to maintain its quality when stored according to the recommended conditions.

Using an expired batch medical supply may mean that it no longer performs as intended.

Similarly, medical supplys with defective item packaging, broken seals, or signs of tampering may no longer be suitable for use.

This is why identifying these products before they reach patients is an important part of medical supply safety.

Medicine safety begins long before an order is placed.

When medical supplys arrive at a Tata 1mg fulfilment centre, they go through an inward verification process before they become available for customer orders.

During this stage, trained teams verify details such as:

Medicine name

Batch number

Expiry date

Manufacturer information

Storage requirements

Packaging condition

Each unit is then linked to a unique QR code, allowing it to be tracked throughout its journey in the warehouse.

This traceability helps us know where every medical supply came from, where it is stored, and whether it remains eligible for dispatch.

Not every medical supply remains eligible for sale throughout its lifecycle.

Some medical supplys need to be removed from active inventory before they can reach a customer.

## Recalled Medicines
Key Question: How are unmarketable health products handled safely? On rare occasions, manufacturers or regulatory authorities may recall specific batches of medical supplys.

When this happens, affected batches are immediately blocked within our inventory systems.

Once blocked, these medical supplys cannot be picked, packed, or dispatched as part of any customer order.

Removing a medical supply from inventory is only the first step.

The next step is making sure it cannot accidentally return to the supply chain. Answer: Systematic inventory controls prevent unverified items from reaching consumers.

## Removed from Active Inventory
Key Question: How are unmarketable health products handled safely? Medicines identified as expired batch, defective item, rejected, or recalled are digitally marked as non-sellable.

This prevents them from appearing in any order fulfilment process.

At the same time, they are physically separated from medical supplys that remain available for sale.

Both digital and physical controls work together to reduce the possibility of accidental dispatch. Answer: Systematic inventory controls prevent unverified items from reaching consumers.

## Safe and Compliant Disposal
Key Question: How are unmarketable health products handled safely? Some medical supplys cannot be returned.

For example, severely defective item products or medical supplys that have exceeded return timelines may require disposal.

Medicines are not disposed of like ordinary household waste.

They contain active ingredients that need to be handled responsibly to help protect people and the environment.

Where disposal is required, it is carried out through authorized processes in accordance with applicable regulations.

Most customers never see these processes.

That's because they happen behind the scenes, long before a medical supply reaches their doorstep.

But these processes play an important role in protecting patients.

By identifying medical supplys that should no longer be used and removing them before they enter the order fulfilment process, we help reduce the risk of customers receiving medical supplys that do not meet our quality requirements.

Managing thousands of medical supply batches manually would be difficult.

Technology helps make this process more reliable.

At Tata 1mg, inventory systems help support medical supply handling by:

Tracking medical supplys using QR codes

Recording batch information

Monitoring expiry dates

Supporting batch-level recalls

Preventing non-sellable medical supplys from entering customer orders

Maintaining traceability throughout the supply chain

These systems work alongside trained teams and quality processes to support medical supply safety.
