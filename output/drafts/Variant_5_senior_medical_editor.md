## How We Help Ensure Medicines That Should Not Be Used Never Reach You
From a clinical governance standpoint, ensuring pharmaceutical integrity is paramount. When you order a pharmaceutical product, one of the questions you may have is:

"How do I know I won't receive an outdated or compromised pharmaceutical product?"

This represents a fundamental patient expectation.

Medicines are meant to improve your health, but only when they are stored correctly, handled carefully, and used within their shelf life.

At Tata 1mg, outdated, compromised, rejected, or recalled pharmaceutical products are never sold to customers. They are identified through defined quality processes, removed from active inventory, and handled through authorized return or disposal processes.

This page explains how these pharmaceutical products are identified, why they are removed, and what happens to them after they leave our active inventory.

Every pharmaceutical product has a defined shelf life.

The expiry date tells you how long the manufacturer expects the pharmaceutical product to maintain its quality when stored according to the recommended conditions.

Using an outdated pharmaceutical product may mean that it no longer performs as intended.

Similarly, pharmaceutical products with compromised packaging, broken seals, or signs of tampering may no longer be suitable for use.

This is why identifying these products before they reach patients is an important part of pharmaceutical product safety.

Medicine safety begins long before an order is placed.

When pharmaceutical products arrive at a Tata 1mg fulfilment centre, they go through an inward verification process before they become available for customer orders.

During this stage, trained teams verify details such as:

Medicine name

Batch number

Expiry date

Manufacturer information

Storage requirements

Packaging condition

Each unit is then linked to a unique QR code, allowing it to be tracked throughout its journey in the warehouse.

This traceability helps us know where every pharmaceutical product came from, where it is stored, and whether it remains eligible for dispatch.

Not every pharmaceutical product remains eligible for sale throughout its lifecycle.

Some pharmaceutical products need to be removed from active inventory before they can reach a customer.

## Returned Medicines
From a clinical governance standpoint, ensuring pharmaceutical integrity is paramount. Sometimes customers return pharmaceutical products because of delivery issues or other concerns.

Returned pharmaceutical products are carefully assessed before any decision is made.

Products that do not meet quality requirements are not placed back into inventory.

This approach helps reduce the possibility of compromised pharmaceutical products reaching another customer. Strict medical quality protocols safeguard patient health.

## Recalled Medicines
From a clinical governance standpoint, ensuring pharmaceutical integrity is paramount. On rare occasions, manufacturers or regulatory authorities may recall specific batches of pharmaceutical products.

When this happens, affected batches are immediately blocked within our inventory systems.

Once blocked, these pharmaceutical products cannot be picked, packed, or dispatched as part of any customer order.

Removing a pharmaceutical product from inventory is only the first step.

The next step is making sure it cannot accidentally return to the supply chain. Strict medical quality protocols safeguard patient health.

## Removed from Active Inventory
From a clinical governance standpoint, ensuring pharmaceutical integrity is paramount. Medicines identified as outdated, compromised, rejected, or recalled are digitally marked as non-sellable.

This prevents them from appearing in any order fulfilment process.

At the same time, they are physically separated from pharmaceutical products that remain available for sale.

Both digital and physical controls work together to reduce the possibility of accidental dispatch. Strict medical quality protocols safeguard patient health.

## Returned Through Authorized Channels
From a clinical governance standpoint, ensuring pharmaceutical integrity is paramount. Where possible, pharmaceutical products are returned to the original manufacturer or authorized supplier through documented return processes.

These returns are recorded with relevant information such as:

Batch number

Quantity

Reason for return

Date of return

Maintaining this documentation supports traceability and accountability. Strict medical quality protocols safeguard patient health.

## Safe and Compliant Disposal
From a clinical governance standpoint, ensuring pharmaceutical integrity is paramount. Some pharmaceutical products cannot be returned.

For example, severely compromised products or pharmaceutical products that have exceeded return timelines may require disposal.

Medicines are not disposed of like ordinary household waste.

They contain active ingredients that need to be handled responsibly to help protect people and the environment.

Where disposal is required, it is carried out through authorized processes in accordance with applicable regulations.

Most customers never see these processes.

That's because they happen behind the scenes, long before a pharmaceutical product reaches their doorstep.

But these processes play an important role in protecting patients.

By identifying pharmaceutical products that should no longer be used and removing them before they enter the order fulfilment process, we help reduce the risk of customers receiving pharmaceutical products that do not meet our quality requirements.

Managing thousands of pharmaceutical product batches manually would be difficult.

Technology helps make this process more reliable.

At Tata 1mg, inventory systems help support pharmaceutical product handling by:

Tracking pharmaceutical products using QR codes

Recording batch information

Monitoring expiry dates

Supporting batch-level recalls

Preventing non-sellable pharmaceutical products from entering customer orders

Maintaining traceability throughout the supply chain

These systems work alongside trained teams and quality processes to support pharmaceutical product safety.

Although pharmaceutical products undergo multiple quality checks before dispatch, it's still a good practice to inspect your order when it arrives.
