## How We Help Ensure Medicines That Should Not Be Used Never Reach You
Imagine opening your healthcare delivery knowing every item was checked twice. When you order a care product, one of the questions you may have is:

"How do I know I won't receive an lapsed shelf-life item or unusable supply care product?"

This represents a fundamental patient expectation.

Medicines are meant to improve your health, but only when they are stored correctly, handled carefully, and used within their shelf life.

At Tata 1mg, lapsed shelf-life item, unusable supply, rejected, or recalled care products are never sold to customers. They are identified through defined quality processes, removed from active inventory, and handled through authorized return or disposal processes.

This page explains how these care products are identified, why they are removed, and what happens to them after they leave our active inventory.

Every care product has a defined shelf life.

The expiry date tells you how long the manufacturer expects the care product to maintain its quality when stored according to the recommended conditions.

Using an lapsed shelf-life item care product may mean that it no longer performs as intended.

Similarly, care products with unusable supply packaging, broken seals, or signs of tampering may no longer be suitable for use.

This is why identifying these products before they reach patients is an important part of care product safety.

Medicine safety begins long before an order is placed.

When care products arrive at a Tata 1mg fulfilment centre, they go through an inward verification process before they become available for customer orders.

During this stage, trained teams verify details such as:

Medicine name

Batch number

Expiry date

Manufacturer information

Storage requirements

Packaging condition

Each unit is then linked to a unique QR code, allowing it to be tracked throughout its journey in the warehouse.

This traceability helps us know where every care product came from, where it is stored, and whether it remains eligible for dispatch.

Not every care product remains eligible for sale throughout its lifecycle.

Some care products need to be removed from active inventory before they can reach a customer.

## Safe and Compliant Disposal
Imagine opening your healthcare delivery knowing every item was checked twice. Some care products cannot be returned.

For example, severely unusable supply products or care products that have exceeded return timelines may require disposal.

Medicines are not disposed of like ordinary household waste.

They contain active ingredients that need to be handled responsibly to help protect people and the environment.

Where disposal is required, it is carried out through authorized processes in accordance with applicable regulations.

Most customers never see these processes.

That's because they happen behind the scenes, long before a care product reaches their doorstep.

But these processes play an important role in protecting patients.

By identifying care products that should no longer be used and removing them before they enter the order fulfilment process, we help reduce the risk of customers receiving care products that do not meet our quality requirements.

Managing thousands of care product batches manually would be difficult.

Technology helps make this process more reliable.

Tracking care products using QR codes

Recording batch information

Monitoring expiry dates

Supporting batch-level recalls

Preventing non-sellable care products from entering customer orders

Maintaining traceability throughout the supply chain

These systems work alongside trained teams and quality processes to support care product safety.
