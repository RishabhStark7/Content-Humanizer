## How We Help Ensure Medicines That Should Not Be Used Never Reach You
Have you ever wondered what happens behind the scenes before your prescription arrives at your doorstep? When you order a medication, one of the questions you may have is:

"How do I know I won't receive an old or damaged packaging medication?"

This represents a fundamental patient expectation.

Medicines are meant to improve your health, but only when they are stored correctly, handled carefully, and used within their shelf life.

At Tata 1mg, old, damaged packaging, rejected, or recalled medications are never sold to customers. They are identified through defined quality processes, removed from active inventory, and handled through authorized return or disposal processes.

This page explains how these medications are identified, why they are removed, and what happens to them after they leave our active inventory.

Every medication has a defined shelf life.

The expiry date tells you how long the manufacturer expects the medication to maintain its quality when stored according to the recommended conditions.

Using an old medication may mean that it no longer performs as intended.

Similarly, medications with damaged packaging packaging, broken seals, or signs of tampering may no longer be suitable for use.

This is why identifying these products before they reach patients is an important part of medication safety.

Medicine safety begins long before an order is placed.

When medications arrive at a Tata 1mg fulfilment centre, they go through an inward verification process before they become available for customer orders.

During this stage, trained teams verify details such as:

Medicine name

Batch number

Expiry date

Manufacturer information

Storage requirements

Packaging condition

Each unit is then linked to a unique QR code, allowing it to be tracked throughout its journey in the warehouse.

This traceability helps us know where every medication came from, where it is stored, and whether it remains eligible for dispatch.

Not every medication remains eligible for sale throughout its lifecycle.
