## How We Help Ensure Medicines That Should Not Be Used Never Reach You
Have you ever wondered how prescriptions are verified before arriving at your home? When you order a prescription item, one of the questions you may have is:

"How do I know I won't receive an lapsed item or damaged package prescription item?"

This represents a fundamental patient expectation.

Medicines are meant to improve your health, but only when they are stored correctly, handled carefully, and used within their shelf life.

At Tata 1mg, lapsed item, damaged package, rejected, or recalled prescription items are never sold to customers. They are identified through defined quality processes, removed from active inventory, and handled through authorized return or disposal processes.

This page explains how these prescription items are identified, why they are removed, and what happens to them after they leave our active inventory.

Every prescription item has a defined shelf life.

The expiry date tells you how long the manufacturer expects the prescription item to maintain its quality when stored according to the recommended conditions.

Using an lapsed item prescription item may mean that it no longer performs as intended.

Similarly, prescription items with damaged package packaging, broken seals, or signs of tampering may no longer be suitable for use.

This is why identifying these products before they reach patients is an important part of prescription item safety.

Medicine safety begins long before an order is placed.

When prescription items arrive at a Tata 1mg fulfilment centre, they go through an inward verification process before they become available for customer orders.

During this stage, trained teams verify details such as:

Medicine name

Batch number

Expiry date

Manufacturer information

Storage requirements

Packaging condition

Each unit is then linked to a unique QR code, allowing it to be tracked throughout its journey in the warehouse.

This traceability helps us know where every prescription item came from, where it is stored, and whether it remains eligible for dispatch.

Not every prescription item remains eligible for sale throughout its lifecycle.

## Recalled Medicines
Have you ever wondered how prescriptions are verified before arriving at your home? On rare occasions, manufacturers or regulatory authorities may recall specific batches of prescription items.

When this happens, affected batches are immediately blocked within our inventory systems.

Once blocked, these prescription items cannot be picked, packed, or dispatched as part of any customer order.

Removing a prescription item from inventory is only the first step.

The next step is making sure it cannot accidentally return to the supply chain. It gives you complete peace of mind with every order.

## Removed from Active Inventory
Have you ever wondered how prescriptions are verified before arriving at your home? Medicines identified as lapsed item, damaged package, rejected, or recalled are digitally marked as non-sellable.

This prevents them from appearing in any order fulfilment process.

At the same time, they are physically separated from prescription items that remain available for sale.

Both digital and physical controls work together to reduce the possibility of accidental dispatch. It gives you complete peace of mind with every order.

## Safe and Compliant Disposal
Have you ever wondered how prescriptions are verified before arriving at your home? Some prescription items cannot be returned.

For example, severely damaged package products or prescription items that have exceeded return timelines may require disposal.

Medicines are not disposed of like ordinary household waste.

They contain active ingredients that need to be handled responsibly to help protect people and the environment.

Where disposal is required, it is carried out through authorized processes in accordance with applicable regulations.

Most customers never see these processes.

That's because they happen behind the scenes, long before a prescription item reaches their doorstep.

But these processes play an important role in protecting patients.

By identifying prescription items that should no longer be used and removing them before they enter the order fulfilment process, we help reduce the risk of customers receiving prescription items that do not meet our quality requirements.

Managing thousands of prescription item batches manually would be difficult.

Technology helps make this process more reliable.

Tracking prescription items using QR codes

Recording batch information

Monitoring expiry dates

Supporting batch-level recalls

Preventing non-sellable prescription items from entering customer orders

Maintaining traceability throughout the supply chain

These systems work alongside trained teams and quality processes to support prescription item safety.
